WSL repository:
  make enter NODE=service01.bob1.oceanresearch.test
Inside the receiver:
  tail -n 15 /tmp/iperf-server.log

Inspect: offered load from traffic-status, sending-interface throughput and
egress discards, receiving-interface rate and iperf receiver throughput/loss.
Keep the device/interface filters and observation window fixed during comparison.
Decision: a capped receive rate plus growing drops at the sending handoff supports
congestion there. Low receive rate without those drops requires checking sender,
receiver and collector state before attributing it to the cap. Save the 40 Mbit/s
control alongside the 60/75 Mbit/s results.
@@ solution
40 Mbit/s is below the transit cap. At 60/75 Mbit/s the offered load exceeds it.
The sending interface is Lagoon Transit ethernet-1/1 toward ReefNet Edge 01.
Expect delivered payload throughput around or below 50 Mbit/s, not an exact
50 Mbit/s, because overhead, buffering and simulator load affect the measurement.
SR Linux egress discards should increase there. The customer's 100 Mbit/s handoff
has headroom. A five-minute increase can plateau during sustained steady loss.
Congestion can also reduce probe success while routes and sessions stay up.

@@ 3 Cross-check loss without double counting
Corroborate the observed loss with an independent measurement.
Explain whether Linux queue drops and SR Linux discards can be added together.

Start in WSL:
  make traffic-diagnostics
@@ hint
Keep the offered load fixed during the two diagnostic snapshots.
Compare the same interface and direction in both snapshots, then in WSL:
  make enter NODE=edge01.bob1.lagoontransit.test
Inside Lagoon Transit:
  info from state interface ethernet-1/1 statistics
Use quit, then reduce the load in WSL:
  make traffic-10mbit
Do new drops stop accumulating? Compare physical out-discarded-packets, not
the subinterface discard row in show interface detail. Check deltas and resets.
A rolling five-minute panel still includes earlier loss.
@@ investigation
Keep the offered load fixed. In Telemetry Health inspect Capacity queue collection
and Linux queue drops (last 5 min) for Lagoon Transit.
WSL repository:
  make traffic-diagnostics
  make enter NODE=edge01.bob1.lagoontransit.test
Inside the Lagoon router:
  info from state interface ethernet-1/1 statistics
  quit
WSL repository:
  make traffic-10mbit

Inspect: counter deltas between the two saved snapshots and the receiver's loss,
not only an absolute cumulative counter. Match interface, direction and time window.
Decision: consistent sending-queue drops and receiver loss corroborate congestion.
A missing queue sample, reset counter or failed collection invalidates a numeric
comparison. After reducing to 10 Mbit/s, check whether NEW drops stop accumulating.
Retain the diagnostic report filename as incident evidence.
@@ solution
The lab cap is a Linux netem egress queue. Its drops can also appear in the
SR Linux egress counter, so the two values must not be added together.
Normal dashboards show SR Linux discards. Linux queue drops are diagnostic
evidence in Telemetry Health and the saved traffic report under .state/.
The collection streams are sampled separately, so their deltas need not match exactly.
At 10 Mbit/s new congestion drops should stop. Rolling-window panels retain
earlier loss until it ages out, so they need not return to zero immediately.

@@ 6 Compare a short burst with the dashboard's rate window
Can Grafana show a five-second burst? Compare short and long rate windows.

Start in WSL:
  make traffic-stop
  make traffic-burst-40mbit
The burst stops automatically. Keep previous faults cleared.
@@ hint
In WSL:
  make traffic-status
In Grafana, select the time around the burst on Lagoon's transit interface.
Compare a short rate window with a five-minute window. Keep the interface,
direction and units the same. What happens to the peak height?
Check the receiver in WSL:
  make enter NODE=service01.bob1.oceanresearch.test
Inside the service node:
  tail -n 15 /tmp/iperf-server.log
Use exit to return. Scrape timing can affect the visible peak.
@@ investigation
WSL repository:
  make test
Confirm all four results pass, then:
  make traffic-stop
  date -Is
  make traffic-burst-40mbit
After at least five seconds:
  make traffic-status
  make enter NODE=service01.bob1.oceanresearch.test
Inside the service node:
  tail -n 15 /tmp/iperf-server.log
  exit
In Grafana inspect the same Lagoon handoff and the time around the burst.
Compare the short and five-minute rate windows while holding other filters fixed.
Record the burst timestamp, both windows and their displayed peaks.
@@ solution
The source offers 40 Mbit/s for five seconds, then stops. This is below the
50 Mbit/s transit cap. A longer rate window spreads that activity over more
time and lowers the displayed peak. With otherwise idle traffic, its idealized
five-minute average is about 40 * 5 / 300 = 0.67 Mbit/s. The actual plotted value
depends on sampling, window alignment and overhead. The receiver log confirms
traffic independently. A low plotted peak does not mean the burst never happened.
